WEEK-04 — WAREHOUSE OPERATIONS & EFFICIENCY
INSIGHTS REPORT
============================================================

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------
Week-04 analysed warehouse capacity, space utilisation, product-warehouse
alignment, warehouse performance and operational bottleneck risk across
six branches.

The analysis shows significant calculated capacity pressure across all
warehouses. At the same time, the relative warehouse performance model
classifies all six branches as High Performance. This demonstrates that
financial/operational efficiency and physical capacity risk are separate
dimensions that should be monitored together.

2. WAREHOUSE SPACE UTILISATION
------------------------------
All six warehouses were classified as Over Capacity under the calculated
floor-footprint model.

Kolkata East Depot recorded the highest calculated utilisation pressure,
followed by Ahmedabad West Hub and Hyderabad Logistics.

Important limitation:
The dataset does not provide detailed information on racks, pallets,
stacking height, aisles or storage configurations. Therefore, the
calculated utilisation figures should be treated as analytical indicators
rather than literal physical warehouse occupancy.

3. WAREHOUSE CAPACITY RISK
--------------------------
All six branches were classified as Critical under the capacity-risk model.

Kolkata showed the highest calculated capacity pressure, while Delhi
recorded the lowest calculated pressure among the six branches.

This indicates that warehouse capacity should be treated as a major
operational risk area.

4. PRODUCT-WAREHOUSE ALIGNMENT
------------------------------
All six warehouses store 30 products.

Heavy products are major drivers of physical handling pressure.

The largest heavy-stock contributors were:

- P015 — Engine Block EB-600 — 320 kg/unit
- P014 — Transmission Assembly TA-800 — 260 kg/unit
- P004 — Track Chain TC-45 — 210 kg/unit

Chennai had the highest total inventory value at approximately 47.8 billion,
while Delhi had the highest total stock volume at approximately 3.29 million
units.

Chennai also recorded the highest total stock weight at approximately
121.8 million kg.

5. WAREHOUSE PERFORMANCE
------------------------
Relative warehouse performance scores were:

1. Delhi Central — 98.51
2. Chennai South Hub — 94.42
3. Pune Distribution — 94.00
4. Hyderabad Logistics — 88.08
5. Ahmedabad West Hub — 85.73
6. Kolkata East Depot — 85.52

All six warehouses were classified as High Performance.

The score combines cost efficiency, employee productivity, market demand
and capacity efficiency.

6. OPERATIONAL BOTTLENECK RISK
------------------------------
The bottleneck model identified:

- 3 Critical Bottlenecks
- 3 High Bottleneck Risks
- 0 Moderate Bottleneck Risks
- 0 Low Bottleneck Risks

Top bottlenecks:

1. Kolkata East Depot — 99.37
2. Hyderabad Logistics — 90.53
3. Ahmedabad West Hub — 86.60

Pune, Chennai and Delhi were classified as High Bottleneck Risk.

7. KEY BUSINESS INSIGHT
-----------------------
A major finding is that warehouse performance and bottleneck risk do not
produce the same ranking.

Delhi ranked first in overall warehouse performance but had the lowest
bottleneck score among the six warehouses.

Kolkata ranked last in performance but had the highest bottleneck score.

This demonstrates that strong financial/operational efficiency does not
necessarily mean low physical capacity or workload pressure.

8. BUSINESS IMPLICATIONS
------------------------
The organisation should monitor warehouse efficiency and warehouse pressure
as separate but connected KPI dimensions.

Heavy products require particular attention because high stock quantities
combined with high unit weight can create significant handling and space
pressure.

Warehouse-level monitoring should combine:

- Capacity utilisation
- Inventory volume
- Inventory value
- Stock weight
- Employee workload
- Revenue efficiency
- Market demand

9. DATA LIMITATIONS
-------------------
The warehouse utilisation analysis is based on product dimensions and
current stock. It does not include rack height, pallet configuration,
aisle space, stacking rules or actual storage layout.

The performance and bottleneck scores are analytical scoring models
developed from the available dataset and should not be interpreted as
official company KPIs.

============================================================
WEEK-04 CONCLUSION
============================================================

Week-04 establishes a warehouse intelligence layer covering capacity,
product alignment, performance and bottleneck risk.

The strongest portfolio-level insight is that warehouse efficiency and
warehouse capacity risk must be evaluated together rather than using a
single KPI.
